🤖 AI Summary: Agentic robotics, centered on an Embodied System 2 formed by a foundation model and embodied harness, requires a shared evaluation framework and research roadmap to achieve reliable physical autonomy.

📄 Abstract

The release of GPT-6 Astra marks the emergence of a new paradigm in robotics: agentic robotics. Within weeks, frontier models with agentic harnesses matched or exceeded task-specific policies on many embodied benchmarks. Instead of training a dedicated policy for each family of tasks, an agentic robotic system applies a foundation model to open-world embodied tasks. It operates in a closed loop: observing the environment, interpreting user intent, reasoning about the task, invoking skills and tools, verifying outcomes, and adapting its strategy. Despite these advances, the community has yet to converge on a shared definition, a reliable evaluation framework, or a research roadmap. We argue that Embodied System 2, realized by a foundation model together with an embodied harness, forms the decision-making core of agentic robotics. Its central challenge is to translate general-purpose reasoning into effective and verifiable actions under physical constraints such as limited reaction time, mechanical limits, unpredictable environment changes, and irreversible consequences. Motivated by this perspective, we propose an evaluation framework that measures both model capabilities and closed-loop system performance. Furthermore, we identify the principles that carry over from digital to physical agents and the challenges that must be addressed to achieve reliable physical autonomy. On top of the Embodied System 2 and the evaluation framework, we envision a research roadmap toward sustained autonomy: the ability to perform work safely and reliably with progressively broader responsibility, from executing individual primitives to fulfilling standing roles.

📄 Abstract

We present K2-Horizon, a fleet of six fully open language models, from a 0.9B dense model to a 375B-A23B MoE, built for general-purpose reasoning and agentic work across math and science, software engineering, deep research, office automation, and beyond. 📄 Abstract

Real-world portfolio management requires converting noisy market observations into coordinated, simultaneous allocations across multiple assets under shared capital constraints. 📄 Abstract

Controllable real-world video generation models can condition on a rendered graybox proxy video, which fixes layout, motion and camera. Coding agents can build such videos by reconstructing a 4D scene in Blender, yet no benchmark checks both these videos and the long, multi-shot scenes behind them. We introduce VibePrevisBench, a benchmark for 4D scene reconstruction: an agent turns a brief into an executable 4D scene and renders its proxy video. 🤖 AI Summary: Edge0 enables efficient mixture-of-experts inference on consumer hardware by predicting expert routing ahead of time and using recovery adapters to maintain quality with minimal active memory.

📄 Abstract

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) inference on consumer hardware is bounded by weight memory: a 35B-class model is 19.5GB at 4-bit, and sparsity shrinks the compute per token, not the bytes that must be held. Naive offloading to SSD does not help on its own, because layer N+1's experts must be chosen before layer N's output exists, so the reads cannot start early enough to hide behind compute. We present Edge0, a streaming MoE inference engine that closes the gap with a prerouter: a per-layer head predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead, and the prediction is consumed as the routing itself, so the staged expert set equals the routed set and nothing is dropped. An unmerged recovery LoRA, trained on the student path, pays back the quality lost to int4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s inside 3GiB of peak active memory, within a few points of its fp16 teacher on average across five public benchmarks. An 8B tier runs on the same framework, and the framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open source.

SPILLWAY: Separating Target Admission from Sanitization to Defend Tool-Using Agents against Prompt Injection

Published: 2026-11-04
DAILYWEEKLY
📄 PDF 🐙 GitHub
🔥 47
⭐ 2

🤖 AI Summary: (No summary available)

📄 Abstract

Tool-using agents act on email, files, and payments, so an instruction hidden in a web page or a shared document can make an agent send data or money to an attacker. Many runtime defenses put a language model on the decision that lets an action through, so the decision depends on a model that may read the attacker's text and adds model calls to every step. Provenance defenses keep the model off the final check but need a model to plan or route each task first. Where an action goes can instead be read from the tools' typed returns: an email's sender or a payment's account arrives in a typed field, while injected instructions arrive as free text. SPILLWAY turns this observation into a deterministic floor with no per-task plan. The check admits a call when each target value that appears in tool output but not in the user's task was returned by a read-only tool in a typed field or is covered by a fixed delegation rule. A deterministic sanitizer removes instruction-like regions its fixed rules match before the model reads them and retains the removed spans as evidence, and a local judge may restore a refused call, never one whose target lies in the removed text. Across three backbones, and seven task suites on one of them, no standard templated injection redirects an action to an attacker's target under SPILLWAY, at the highest observed mean utility and composite scores (NRP, ERS) of the defenses we run under that attack. A forged chat template also produces a raw zero, and mean in-scope attack success under a released reinforcement-learning attacker is .007. SPILLWAY reaches this floor with no defense-triggered backbone re-run and almost no added model call. As personal AI agents act on users' email, files, and payments, SPILLWAY gives them an auditable refusal floor on where their actions go that keeps them useful and holds across backbones.

📄 Abstract

Real-time long-video generation requires continuous output with consistent appearance and natural motion. Existing chunk-wise autoregressive methods face challenges in both generation efficiency and long-video quality: serialized denoising and history-cache refresh require two forward passes even with one-step denoising, and longer rollouts can degrade quality and cause camera drift. 📄 Abstract

Weight-activation quantization can reduce the memory and compute cost of neural-network inference, but recent methods often require expensive optimization and architecture-specific propagation of basis transforms across layers. We introduce MACaQ, an MXFP4 W4A4 post-training quantization method that treats every linear layer independently. MACaQ applies a per-channel scale and a block-Hadamard transform online to each layer's activations, while absorbing the corresponding inverse transforms into its weights.
