Any Time, Any Space: A World-State-Centric Framework for Agentic Video Generation

⭐ 197

🤖 AI Summary: ATAS improves long-form video consistency by compiling screenplays into referenced shot requests, retrieving spatial and temporal evidence from scene and event memories, and conditionally updating memory after generation.

📄 Abstract

Long-form agentic video generation must turn a screenplay into shots that remain spatially and temporally consistent across nonconsecutive requests. Passing prompts or recent frames between shots does not provide a shared spatial frame for revisiting a scene or a time-indexed record for replaying an earlier event. We present Any Time, Any Space (ATAS), which compiles a screenplay into shot requests with explicit location, camera, and event-time references. Before generation, it retrieves relevant evidence from a scene memory of registered static geometry and an event memory of dynamic observations indexed by scene, event, and time. After generation, ATAS reconstructs and registers the clip. It updates memory only when review and state checks accept the observation, while film delivery remains independent of memory admission. Across four video generators, ATAS improves all reported cross-shot consistency measures and achieves the highest aggregate benchmark score for each backbone. It also outperforms the compared agents and 3D-guided methods on target-image spatial revisit, leads three event-recapture criteria, and receives the highest human ratings on all six criteria. The video results and code are available on our project page: https://lhmd.top/atas/.

Beyond Domain-Routed Supervision: Cross-Teacher Guarding for Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

DAILYMONTHLYWEEKLY
📄 PDF
No GitHub
🔥 223
⭐ 0

🤖 AI Summary: Road MOPD reduces gradient conflicts in multi-teacher distillation by using cross-teacher agreement for token-level credit assignment and auxiliary gradients to constrain conflicting updates.

📄 Abstract

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) extends on-policy distillation (OPD) by routing domain-specific data to specialized teachers, integrating their capabilities through dense token-level supervision on student-generated trajectories. However, domain routing only distinguishes the supervision sources for different domains; updates from all domains still act on shared parameters. When teachers differ substantially in response style and domain expertise, supervision that is effective in isolation may still lead to persistent gradient conflicts and capability degradation during joint optimization. To address this challenge, we propose Cross-Teacher Guarding for MOPD (Road MOPD), which introduces non-routed teachers as auxiliary cross-domain critics on the same student trajectories and coordinates multi-teacher supervision through token-level credit assignment and gradient updates. Specifically, cross-teacher-guided credit assignment uses teacher agreement to strengthen token-level corrections jointly supported by the teachers and attenuate conflicting signals; preventive multi-teacher gradient projection uses auxiliary gradients from the same trajectories to constrain potentially conflicting updates and adaptively controls projection strength to retain target-domain learning signals. In experiments, Road MOPD mitigates degradation in the disadvantaged domain, gains over 5 percentage points under high conflict, and improves overall performance in low-conflict and three-domain settings.

False Frontiers: Diagnosing and Mitigating Co-Cheating in Self-Evolving Search Agents

🤖 AI Summary: CrossFit mitigates co-cheating in self-evolving search agents by cross-fitting proposal scoring across partitioned sources, substantially reducing false agreement and improving benchmark performance.

📄 Abstract

Self-evolving search agents build their own training curricula by jointly optimizing a proposer that generates questions and a solver that answers them. This closed loop introduces a failure mode we call co-cheating: the proposer and solver increasingly agree on shared errors, so internal reward improves without a matching gain in external correctness. A post-hoc audit against source evidence shows co-cheating growing more severe over successive rounds of self-evolution, with pseudo-label correctness stagnating or declining even as the in-loop training signal improves. The most direct mitigation is to verify proposals before training: we introduce multi-sample verification (MSV), which queries the same model three times with the source and three times without it to decide task admission and replace unreliable pseudo-labels. MSV partially reduces false agreement but leaves substantial residual co-cheating and costs six extra labeler generations per candidate. These limitations motivate CrossFit, our main method: it partitions the proposer's source documents into groups A and B; questions generated from A are scored by an auxiliary solver trained only on B, and vice versa. The cross-fitted agreement determines proposer reward, so a same-source pseudo-label cannot be reproduced through the feedback solver, while the original solver's update rule is unchanged. Rerunning the loop with Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.5-9B, MSV reduces false-agreement mass from 6.1% to 5.7% and from 8.8% to 7.2%, whereas CrossFit reduces it to 3.0% and 3.7%. Replaying identical proposals with source-excluded feedback further reduces false agreement to 0.4% and 0.1%, isolating feedback ancestry from curriculum changes. Across seven downstream search benchmarks, CrossFit improves average performance over standard coupled self-evolution by 8.8 and 8.4 points and over Search-R1 by 8.7 and 7.8 points at 4B and 9B.

🤖 AI Summary: Doc2Harness is a deterministic two-stage benchmark that evaluates long-horizon harness engineering by having models build and run harnesses across isolated containers, revealing substantial performance gaps among frontier models.

📄 Abstract

Agents driven by large language models are increasingly deployed on long-horizon tasks, where they use tools and modify real workspaces. How well they perform depends as much on the harness around the model as on the model itself. Existing benchmarks measure downstream task success under a fixed harness, leaving harness engineering itself unexplored. We propose Doc2Harness, a two-stage benchmark that evaluates long-horizon harness engineering directly. In Stage 1, a build model operating inside a fixed builder harness receives an interface-only contract skeleton and a development document that consolidates behavior across twelve frontier harnesses, and then constructs a complete candidate harness in an isolated container. In Stage 2, the frozen candidate harness runs in containers separate from the construction environment, one per test case, and is driven end to end by a scripted mock model. We score each test case using four observable channels, namely the requests sent to the model, the files on disk, the process exit code, and the command output; positive controls rule out vacuous passes. Across nineteen frontier models, each evaluated with three independent builds on 328 test cases, GPT-6 Astra achieves the highest pass rate at 53.05%, while the weakest model reaches 27.44%, a gap of roughly twenty-six percentage points. Even the strongest model fails nearly half of the test cases, and failures cluster around how cross-cutting functionality is split across modules. To our knowledge, Doc2Harness provides the first deterministic benchmark for measuring long-horizon harness engineering.

🔥 316

🤖 AI Summary: DiffusionART improves reinforcement learning fine-tuning of diffusion models by removing hidden consistency forces and enabling stable, efficient training without EMA tuning or differentiable rewards.

📄 Abstract

Online reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tunes diffusion models with black-box rewards, but reverse-process methods such as Flow-GRPO are limited in efficiency by poor credit assignment, while forward-process methods such as DiffusionNFT are efficient yet unstable without carefully tuned exponential-moving-average (EMA) off-policy training. We show that NFT-style objectives contain a hidden forward--reverse consistency force that joint positive--negative training cancels only partially, and that EMA stabilizes training through restoration rather than off-policy rollouts. We introduce DiffusionART (Diffusion Reinforcement with Aggregation-First Reward Tilting), which aggregates each prompt's rewards into a shared reward-improving direction for reverse-process states, removing the consistency force by construction, and adds a trust-region-masked second update for drift-adaptive restoration without EMA tuning. It requires no differentiable reward model, remains effective with fewer samples per prompt, and supports pairwise reward models (DiffusionART-Pairwise). Across image and video generation, DiffusionART trains efficiently and stably without per-task tuning. In particular, on HunyuanVideo-13B with equally weighted visual and motion quality rewards, DiffusionART reaches the late-stage mean reward levels of DiffusionNFT and Flow-GRPO-Fast with 12.02 and 11.72 times fewer GPU hours, respectively, while achieving 28.8% and 53.0% higher mean training rewards.

The Teacher Is a Direction, Not a Destination: Extrapolating RL-Induced Representation Residuals in On-Policy Distillation

Published: 2026-09-29
MONTHLY
📄 PDF 🐙 GitHub
🔥 561
⭐ 7

🤖 AI Summary: RIDE improves on-policy distillation by extrapolating reinforcement-learning-induced representation shifts directly in hidden states, yielding stable gains over RL-trained teachers.

📄 Abstract

On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student to match the teacher's next-token distributions on the student's own trajectories and has yielded substantial empirical gains. Generalized variants allow the student to surpass the teacher by extrapolating an implicit reward in output space. The language-model head, however, attenuates this change anisotropically: much of the change encoded in the teacher's hidden states reaches the logits at a small fraction of its weight, and the sampled-token log-probability ratios on which output-space extrapolation relies inject noise that the extrapolation amplifies, making training unstable. We observe that reinforcement learning (RL) shifts a model's internal representations relative to its base checkpoint, and that the direction of this shift can be measured at every layer. Motivated by this observation, we propose RIDE (RL-Induced Direction Extrapolation), which extrapolates the RL-induced change directly in representation space: at every layer and token position, RIDE computes the residual between the teacher and its pre-RL checkpoint and regresses the student's hidden states toward targets displaced beyond the teacher along this residual. Conditioned on a sampled trajectory, this regression is equivalent to maximizing a linear directional reward defined by the residual under a quadratic penalty centered at the teacher, which makes explicit how the objective moves the student along the RL-induced direction while limiting its deviation from the teacher. Across four base/RL-teacher pairs spanning different scales, architectures, and pre-training lineages, RIDE approaches or exceeds the RL-trained teacher on every pair and is the only method whose mean does so, and it consistently outperforms output-space extrapolation, which degrades the student whenever the teacher is close to its base. Project page: https://github.com/xixixixixxxx/RIDE.

🤖 AI Summary: CriticOPD improves on-policy distillation by using teacher critiques to restore recoverability, enabling students to revise incorrect reasoning and achieve stronger mathematical reasoning performance.

📄 Abstract

On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student model on its own rollouts with token-level supervision from a teacher, assuming that the teacher can reach a correct answer from any prefix the student writes. We call this property the recoverability of the teacher and show it fails on incorrect rollouts: the teacher follows student errors, although it recognizes them. We propose CriticOPD, using error recognition to restore recoverability. The teacher critiques an incorrect rollout, allowing the student to rewrite its solution following the error. The resulting output, including the critique as privileged information, is scored by the teacher while the student uses the revision without the critique for training. On two Qwen3 students and six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, CriticOPD improves OPD, with the largest gains on competition problems.

📄 Abstract

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) inference on consumer hardware is bounded by weight memory: a 35B-class model is 19.5GB at 4-bit, and sparsity shrinks the compute per token, not the bytes that must be held. 📄 Abstract

Multimodal language models (MLLMs) exhibit promising spatial reasoning capabilities, yet the internal mechanisms supporting them remain poorly understood. 🤖 AI Summary: ReMaRk improves reinforcement learning for recommendation by scoring generated lists against all valid replies jointly, reducing training cost while matching or exceeding prior performance.

📄 Abstract

A recommendation request on Reddit draws several replies, each naming different titles. When an LLM recommender is trained on such threads with reinforcement learning (RL), which replies should a generated list be rewarded against? The state-of-the-art recipe, Rank-GRPO, picks one reply per training example, so the advantage mostly records which reply was drawn (85% of its variance) rather than how good the list is. We argue that RL should train on the request, not on each answer, and propose ReMaRk: it scores every list once against the union of all replies' titles and samples each request equally. This makes RL cheaper. On seven backbones and three datasets, ReMaRk reaches the validation recall of 2,000 per-reply RL steps in 25 to 53% of the steps and leads at that budget; trained until neither improves, it is never significantly behind Rank-GRPO and in four of seven pairs reaches Rank-GRPO's best with fewer GPU-hours. The same reward helps on ProtoQA, where about 100 people answer each question, which points to a simple rule for RL with several valid human answers: reward them jointly.

📄 Abstract

How can we build an agent that thinks beyond human language, yet still acts through it in the external environment? Modern LLM agents still reason and interact through text, while latent reasoning offers an efficient internal medium but lacks native tool access and reasoning transparency. We bridge these two directions with Hybrid Reasoning Agents (HybridAgents), which dynamically navigate between latent reasoning and text-based interaction throughout a unified generation process.
