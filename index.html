Route Anything: A Study of Model and Policy Routing for General-Purpose AI Systems

Published: 2026-11-03
DAILYMONTHLYWEEKLY
📄 PDF 🐙 GitHub
🔥 87
⭐ 2

🤖 AI Summary: Route Anything is a framework for dynamically selecting and coordinating specialized AI models and policies across diverse tasks to improve performance and efficiency.

📄 Abstract

Artificial general intelligence (AGI) requires a method that can understand, generate, reason, and act across diverse domains and modalities. Rather than relying on a single model to master every capability, a promising path toward AGI now is to orchestrate a heterogeneous ecosystem of specialized models, tools, and policies (candidates) into a unified AI system. At the heart of this vision lies a fundamental question: how can an AI system dynamically select and coordinate the most capable candidate for each task while balancing performance and computational cost? In this work, we present Route Anything, a unified framework for studying model and policy routing across general-purpose AI systems. We develop a unified theoretical foundation that characterizes optimal routing under quality--cost trade-offs and establishes how the information available to a router determines its attainable performance. To systematically investigate routing across diverse capabilities, we first construct an omni-modal trajectory matrix spanning 25 benchmarks across language understanding, image, video, and speech generation, coding and workplace agents, and robotic manipulation, comprising 374,401 scored runs from 452 candidate configurations. Building on this framework, we train and test different routers to investigate the potential of omni-modal model routing, the capabilities of learned routers, and the conditions under which routing improves efficiency. Our results reveal substantial opportunities for capability orchestration: per-input oracle routing outperforms the best fixed candidate by 5.4 points, and learned routers outperform it by 0.3 points across 16 benchmarks, by 2.1 points on video generation, and by 1.1 points on robot manipulation while using 24% less policy time. More importantly, routing raises efficiency by 8.82x on average compared with the reference model.

🤖 AI Summary: TokenEconomizer reduces recurring token costs of coding agents across sequential real-world tasks by pruning irrelevant tools, injecting micro-skills, and interrupting failure loops.

📄 Abstract

Advances in frontier coding agents have sharply increased the token costs of AI-assisted software engineering. Their token efficiency is typically evaluated on isolated tasks, whereas real-world developers use them to continuously build, update, and maintain large-scale projects. This mismatch motivates us to evaluate agents over sequences of realistic coding tasks, where we find that agents exhibit recurring environment-dependent token costs that existing methods struggle to reduce. To minimize these costs, we introduce TokenEconomizer, an external controller that cuts environment-specific token costs over a sequence of tasks. These cuts come from determining (i) which operational micro-skills to inject, (ii) which persistently irrelevant tools to prune, and (iii) when to intervene in unproductive failure loops. Across Claude Code, Codex, and GitHub Copilot CLI paired with frontier models, TokenEconomizer reduces the average token cost by 18.4-30.9% with comparable task success. Savings hold in every repository under every tested agent-model setting and generalize to diverse domains such as sequential web development and security auditing, where costs fall by 6.4-21.2% depending on task structure. Our work establishes adaptive black-box cost control as a promising direction for reducing token costs of coding agents in realistic settings.

📄 Abstract

Psychotherapy clients may experience distress before they can coherently describe it, and their expression changes with emotion, perceived safety, and trust. Large language model-based client simulators often rely on clinically organized case descriptions, which can encourage responses explaining the client's difficulties more clearly than their current understanding warrants. We propose Pandora, a psychotherapy client simulation framework that models how psychological experience becomes situated, individual expression. Pandora updates psychological and relational states in response to therapist interventions, constructs subjective appraisals and attributions, and plans response content and strategy. These results support explicit expression planning in client simulation to improve alignment with individual language patterns and reduce perceived AI-like expression in the evaluated setting.

🤖 AI Summary: Wave Forcing enables efficient continuous long-video generation with consistent appearance and natural motion through co-designed generation, training, and execution, achieving high throughput and quality.

📄 Abstract

Real-time long-video generation requires continuous output with consistent appearance and natural motion. Existing chunk-wise autoregressive methods face challenges in both generation efficiency and long-video quality: serialized denoising and history-cache refresh require two forward passes even with one-step denoising, and longer rollouts can degrade quality and cause camera drift. We propose Wave Forcing (WF), which co-designs generation, training, and execution for continuous video output with multi-step denoising. WF uses chunk-wise causal attention to access earlier, cleaner chunks before their denoising is complete, and distributes staggered denoising and cache-refresh stages across full-model replicas to achieve 1F1C: one output chunk per concurrent forward round after pipeline fill. To adapt the model to this continuous generation process, we develop a three-stage training procedure that successively introduces mixed-noise denoising, motion supervision, and causal adaptation using self-generated history. To execute this process efficiently, Wave Runtime (WaveRT) uses Wave Parallelism (WP) for early K/V delivery and communication-computation overlap, together with optimized kernels and pipelined VAE decoding. Experiments on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B show that 5-step WF maintains overall quality comparable to Rolling Forcing while achieving higher Dynamic Degree on both short and long videos. Our system efficiency experiments show that 4-step WF achieves 147.6 DiT FPS and 134.8 end-to-end FPS on eight H200 GPUs, yielding 7.07x the single-GPU end-to-end throughput. In DiT-only tests with matched SageAttention+W8A8 backends, denoising steps, and GPU counts, WP delivers up to 2.53x the throughput of the faster of the Ulysses and Ring context-parallel baselines across six model scales (1.3B-14B).

🤖 AI Summary: LoCo uses local preference learning to teach reasoning models when to stop deliberating, reducing output length substantially while preserving or improving accuracy across math and code tasks.

📄 Abstract

Large reasoning models achieve strong performance on challenging tasks, but their long chain-of-thought often incurs substantial inference costs by continuing to deliberate after sufficient information has been obtained. Existing reasoning compression methods primarily operate on reasoning trajectories, paying less attention to the local decision of whether further reasoning is necessary given the current state. We propose LoCo, a local preference learning method for reasoning compression. LoCo detects candidate overthinking states from the model's own traces, freezes the corresponding reasoning prefixes, regenerates shorter verified continuations from the same states, and trains on the resulting state-conditioned preference pairs with DPO. This formulation teaches the model when to continue reasoning and when to conclude, rather than simply encouraging shorter trajectories overall. We construct training data entirely from self-generated mathematical traces and evaluate LoCo on five mathematical and three code generation benchmarks. LoCo reduces completion length by 41.7\%--45.4\% on mathematics and 23.4\%--36.3\% on code, while maintaining or improving accuracy and achieving the best AUC across all code benchmarks. LoCo consistently improves the accuracy-efficiency trade-off on mathematics and transfers effectively to code generation despite math-only training, demonstrating the cross-domain generality of local preference learning for reasoning compression. The code and model will be publicly released at https://github.com/MuyaoYuan/LoCo.

🤖 AI Summary: Co-designing a resource-heavy reasoning system with a lightweight predictive evaluator can reduce execution costs by filtering interactions, with efficiency governed by the density of useful interactions.

📄 Abstract

Progress toward artificial general intelligence (AGI) has largely relied on scaling a resource-intensive executive, System-2, through larger models, more data and compute, longer reasoning, and more agents. We ask whether the next stage may come instead from co-designing it with System-1, a lightweight, semantically aware predictor that evaluates candidate interactions before execution, so that System-2 acts only on those worth their cost. Our answer is a qualified yes. We study conditional interaction, a predict--gate--execute--update loop: System-1 estimates each candidate's utility, System-2 executes those admitted by an adaptive threshold, and the outcomes update System-1. The loop spans training, test-time reasoning, adaptive decision-making, and agentic tool use, with utility and cost defined by the application. Under ideal screening and equal execution costs, execution compute falls by a factor approaching 1/ρ_u, where ρ_u is the density of utility-relevant interactions (cost-weighted when costs differ); prediction errors, exploration, and distribution shift reduce the saving. We bound selection regret by predictor error and the utility margin. These results motivate our central hypothesis: ρ_u is a measurable coordinate of scaling efficiency and makes informative compute a better description of scaling than total compute.

📄 Abstract

In recent years, agentic orchestration has been widely applied to solve complex human tasks. However, existing orchestration learning still faces key challenges, including hand-designed curricula, advantage collapse when a task's rollouts all reach the same outcome, and unreliable evaluation signals that judge each skill by one noisy score. To address these challenges, we propose R^2Orch, a new paradigm of Recursive Self-Play Orchestration: an inner loop of self-play that improves the orchestrator on self-chosen tasks, and an outer loop that improves how the inner loop improves.
