Envisioning Agentic Robotics: A New Era of Robotic Systems

🤖 AI Summary: Agentic robotics, centered on an Embodied System 2 formed by a foundation model and embodied harness, requires a shared evaluation framework and research roadmap to achieve reliable physical autonomy.

📄 Abstract

The release of GPT-6 Astra marks the emergence of a new paradigm in robotics: agentic robotics. Within weeks, frontier models with agentic harnesses matched or exceeded task-specific policies on many embodied benchmarks. Instead of training a dedicated policy for each family of tasks, an agentic robotic system applies a foundation model to open-world embodied tasks. It operates in a closed loop: observing the environment, interpreting user intent, reasoning about the task, invoking skills and tools, verifying outcomes, and adapting its strategy. Despite these advances, the community has yet to converge on a shared definition, a reliable evaluation framework, or a research roadmap. We argue that Embodied System 2, realized by a foundation model together with an embodied harness, forms the decision-making core of agentic robotics. Its central challenge is to translate general-purpose reasoning into effective and verifiable actions under physical constraints such as limited reaction time, mechanical limits, unpredictable environment changes, and irreversible consequences. Motivated by this perspective, we propose an evaluation framework that measures both model capabilities and closed-loop system performance. Furthermore, we identify the principles that carry over from digital to physical agents and the challenges that must be addressed to achieve reliable physical autonomy. On top of the Embodied System 2 and the evaluation framework, we envision a research roadmap toward sustained autonomy: the ability to perform work safely and reliably with progressively broader responsibility, from executing individual primitives to fulfilling standing roles.

🤖 AI Summary: Gamer is a generalist agent framework that integrates perception, memory, and multi-step reasoning, trained through continued pre-training, sparse-thinking instruction tuning, and multi-turn reinforcement learning to interact efficiently in 2D and 3D environments.

📄 Abstract

We introduce Gamer, a generalist agent framework built for long-horizon tasks in complex interactive environments. Its end-to-end architecture combines perception, memory management, and multi-step reasoning. A shared training and deployment pipeline supports interaction with both conventional 2D graphical interfaces and complex 3D simulated worlds. We first continue pre-training on a dataset constructed from tens of thousands of hours of device-level keyboard and mouse interaction. These trajectories span simulated games and embodied environments, allowing the model to learn environmental dynamics across both physical and virtual domains. Sparse-thinking instruction tuning then substantially reduces redundant computation while preserving the reasoning needed for complex decisions. We train the policy for long-horizon interaction with a stable multi-turn reinforcement learning method that addresses finite context windows, accumulated rollout errors, and sparse rewards. For real-time interaction, a cloud-edge inference engine serves numerous simulated game environments concurrently, supports unbounded-context execution, and keeps online inference latency low. Across multiple challenging 3D open-world games, Gamer demonstrates solid generalization and operational efficiency comparable to skilled players. It also achieves competitive results on a range of conventional 2D game benchmarks. These results suggest that even repetitive keyboard and mouse trajectories can support substantial gains from scaling data and learning algorithms. Training on these trajectories helps Gamer learn complex skills more steadily in environments with both physical and digital properties. This approach offers a path toward generalist agents that can interact across digital and physical worlds.

The Other Half of the Memory Wall: Serving 35B MoEs from SSD with Trained Routing Prediction

Published: 2026-09-16
TRENDING
📄 PDF 🐙 GitHub
🔥 31
⭐ 11.7k

🤖 AI Summary: Edge0 enables efficient mixture-of-experts inference on consumer hardware by predicting expert routing ahead of time and using recovery adapters to maintain quality with minimal active memory.

📄 Abstract

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) inference on consumer hardware is bounded by weight memory: a 35B-class model is 19.5GB at 4-bit, and sparsity shrinks the compute per token, not the bytes that must be held. Naive offloading to SSD does not help on its own, because layer N+1's experts must be chosen before layer N's output exists, so the reads cannot start early enough to hide behind compute. We present Edge0, a streaming MoE inference engine that closes the gap with a prerouter: a per-layer head predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead, and the prediction is consumed as the routing itself, so the staged expert set equals the routed set and nothing is dropped. An unmerged recovery LoRA, trained on the student path, pays back the quality lost to int4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s inside 3GiB of peak active memory, within a few points of its fp16 teacher on average across five public benchmarks. An 8B tier runs on the same framework, and the framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open source.

Loopback Transformer: Towards a Unified View of Residual Looped Transformers

Published: 2026-11-04
DAILYWEEKLY

🔥 11
⭐ 3

🤖 AI Summary: A looped Transformer that separately controls input strength, total update weight, and per-update allocation achieves lower validation loss and higher downstream accuracy than standard and looped residual baselines while using fewer parameters.

📄 Abstract

Looped Transformers reduce parameter count by repeatedly applying shared blocks, but parameter sharing alone does not determine how the results of repeated computation are reused. We take the depth-memory view of residual connections and analyze which representation is stored after each block application and how later reads weight it. A full block output contains both its input and its new update, so weighting that output also reweights the history already carried by the input. Reading individual updates instead, with the shared stack's input kept on a separate path, gives separate controls over input strength, total update weight, and allocation across updates. The Loopback Transformer implements these controls: geometric decay sets the total update weight and supplies a recency prior, and attention learns how to allocate that weight. On ClimbMix, Loopback reaches lower validation loss than standard Transformer baselines with 40-75% of their Transformer parameters (excluding token embeddings and the output head) at reference scales from 191M to 3B, with training tokens and executed depth matched. It also has higher mean accuracy over thirteen downstream tasks at 1.4B and 3B. At 191M, 374M, and 778M, it has the lowest validation loss and the highest thirteen-task mean among the standard Transformer and seven looped residual designs.

📄 Abstract

Foundation models trained on large unlabeled corpora develop emergent capabilities, tasks that go beyond the training objective. We extend this paradigm to multimodal biology with Bonbon, a foundation model for molecular interactions trained on approximately 3.2 trillion tokens of paired protein-ligand sequences. From a single representation space, Bonbon exhibits three zero-shot emergent capabilities at hierarchical levels of resolution: functional (mechanism of action), residue (binding site localization, including orthosteric/allosteric discrimination), and bond (covalent versus non-covalent engagement). 📄 Abstract

Direct diffusion modeling of high-dimensional raw video data is challenging, and existing pixel-space methods often rely on specialized architectures. We introduce JVT (Just Video Transformer), a simple large-patch DiT-style Transformer for pixel-space video diffusion, without a pretrained autoencoder, auxiliary detailers, cascaded stages, or U-Net-style skip connections. 📄 Abstract

Advances in frontier coding agents have sharply increased the token costs of AI-assisted software engineering. 🤖 AI Summary: A history-aware benchmark and memory-powered method improve Text-to-SQL accuracy and reduce cost by reusing verified SQL components and consulting schemas on demand.

📄 Abstract

Text-to-SQL systems translate natural language questions into SQL queries over increasingly complex databases. However, widely used benchmarks evaluate questions independently, overlooking settings where earlier queries over the same database can supply joins or calculations for a new question. We introduce HARE (History-AwaRe Text-to-SQL BEnchmark) to measure the accuracy and cost of adapting historical SQL. HARE comprises 1,600 requests derived from a fixed pool of 823 verified queries across four datasets. Annotations record which components each request preserves and which edits it requires, distinguishing whole-query adaptation from partial reuse. Our method, MEM-SQL (Memory-EMpowered Text-to-SQL), stores whole-query templates, reusable SQL blocks, and mappings from domain concepts to schema elements. MEM-SQL uses a layer only if every requirement assigned to it has a match above that layer's threshold. With On-demand Schema Linking (OSL), MEM-SQL consults the schema only when no layer qualifies or the generator judges retrieved evidence insufficient. Across six language models on HARE, mean execution accuracy rises from 37.1% for Direct Generation without history to 50.0% for MEM-SQL, with 76.6% fewer input tokens and 68.8% lower latency on average. Further analysis shows that requests reusing several verified queries remain a bottleneck, with accuracy falling from 56.7% to 29.7%, most sharply when reused components must be placed in a new query structure or combined with a shared join. Applying OSL to three other memory designs consistently reduces generation cost, while its accuracy benefit depends on the memory design. Verified history can thus make generation both more accurate and cheaper, but its value depends on the evidence retained and when the system consults the schema.

🤖 AI Summary: StepRefine, a plug-and-play decoding strategy, mitigates hallucinations in multimodal diffusion language models by switching between discrete denoising and latent refinement based on entropy spikes and distributional stability.

📄 Abstract

Recent advancements in multimodal diffusion language models (MDLMs) have exhibited strong global modeling capabilities through iterative refinement. We observe that mask uncertainty follows an overall downward trend during iterative denoising, while a few denoising steps exhibit localized entropy spikes closely associated with hallucinations. We argue that anomalous steps can be identified by measuring the entropy deviation of each denoising step from its neighboring temporal window. We hypothesize that discrete token commitment discards the distributional information at masked positions, limiting the continued interaction between candidate semantics and the context during entropy spikes. With this goal, we present StepRefine, a plug-and-play decoding strategy that leverages semantic context to achieve reliable denoising. The core of our method lies in a step-aware switch between discrete denoising and latent refinement. The model employs probability-weighted embeddings at entropy spike steps to preserve diverse reasoning hypotheses and performs multiple rounds of continuous refinement. Meanwhile, a refinement controller monitors distributional stability to reduce unnecessary loops and switch the model back to standard discrete denoising. Through extensive experiments, StepRefine demonstrates significant hallucination mitigation across different MDLMs on multimodal benchmarks.

🤖 AI Summary: Wave Forcing enables efficient continuous long-video generation with consistent appearance and natural motion through co-designed generation, training, and execution, achieving high throughput and quality.

📄 Abstract

Real-time long-video generation requires continuous output with consistent appearance and natural motion. Existing chunk-wise autoregressive methods face challenges in both generation efficiency and long-video quality: serialized denoising and history-cache refresh require two forward passes even with one-step denoising, and longer rollouts can degrade quality and cause camera drift. 🤖 AI Summary: A streaming speaker-diarization method removes the fixed speaker-slot limit by clustering speaker embeddings within slots after all slots are used, allowing unlimited speakers and efficient large-scale linking.

📄 Abstract

Meetings last up to an hour, and nobody knows in advance how many people will speak. Neural diarizers with a fixed number of output slots, such as Streaming Sortformer and Nemotron-3, are accurate and run online, but they cannot name more speakers than they have slots.
