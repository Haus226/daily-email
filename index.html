ACAP: Asynchronous Coding Agents for Efficient Robot Control

Published: 2026-11-23
DAILYWEEKLY
📄 PDF
No GitHub
🔥 47
⭐ 0

🤖 AI Summary: (No summary available)

📄 Abstract

Coding agents can construct and revise robot behavior during execution, but using one agent for the whole robot can delay otherwise independent work. We introduce Asynchronous Coding Agents as Policy (ACAP), an asynchronous framework that organizes one robot as a team of persistent agents. Each member reasons and acts for a configurable control group, such as an arm with its gripper or the torso and chassis, enabling asynchronous reasoning and execution. However, different robot components often have physical dependencies that require coordination. To address this challenge, ACAP introduces a hyper-agent that assigns tasks, coordinates dependencies, and dynamically allocates control between the lead and member agents. This allows independent work to progress concurrently while supporting tightly coupled manipulation when necessary. Across 72 real-world rollouts covering 25 task/platform settings, ACAP reduces task-balanced time cost by 28.2% compared with a single-agent Direct Policy, with improvements across Fully Parallel, Parallel-Sequential, and Collaborative tasks. Across all eight Collaborative settings, ACAP also achieves lower mean time cost than Fixed Delegation, where only member agents can control their assigned groups. An additional 90-rollout study shows lower mean 30-minute-capped time cost than Direct on all six tasks. We further demonstrate mobile manipulation using the same member interface for chassis and torso control. We present the framework and a real-world rollout dataset containing observations, generated programs, actions, and feedback to support research on how coding agents reason and act in the physical world.

Motion Mamba 2.0: Autoregressive Diffusion Mamba for Long-Horizon Humanoid Control

Published: 2026-11-23

📄 Abstract

Long-horizon, real-time controllable motion generation is essential for interactive animation and humanoid robotics, where an agent must respond to changing instructions and spatial goals while moving. Existing autoregressive diffusion methods generate motion incrementally, but bounded attention caches limit access to distant history, while repeated denoising introduces substantial inference overhead. We present Motion Mamba 2.0, a Mamba-based root-body diffusion architecture that integrates local attention, resumable recurrent states, and persistent content memory (PCM) for long-horizon controllable motion generation. PCM transforms compressed motion history from Mamba recurrent states, together with state changes and text-conditioned features, into dynamically updated content slots accessible to local attention. To enable efficient inference, we further develop an execution scheme tailored to this architecture, combining CUDA Graph replay, fused kernels, and execution-flow simplification while preserving the canonical history update. Evaluations on retargeted HiPHI demonstrate accurate terminal control over rollouts of up to 39.52 s, while short-horizon comparisons on BONES Seed and retargeted HumanML3D show that the model also retains strong spatial control and text-motion matching. On an RTX 4090, the optimized execution reduces median complete-window latency from 1685.35 to 276.14 ms, achieving a median paired speedup of 6.138times while preserving identical outputs and committed states in the tested rollouts. Moreover, we conduct extensive real-world evaluation on the Unitree G1, demonstrating sequential text-guided action composition, expressive motion, and interactive locomotion through keyboard and mouse control.

📄 Abstract

We propose loom attention, which allows a Transformer to dynamically rewire its circuits at every token by interweaving a head's vertical attention over the outputs of earlier heads (to obtain its query, key and value) with its horizontal attention over tokens. We instantiate vertical attention efficiently with normalized and gated linear attention over a per-token matrix memory, which stores the compressed head outputs and serves as a second residual stream. 🤖 AI Summary: Unlimited OCR introduces Reference Sliding Window Attention to eliminate growing memory consumption during long-sequence OCR tasks, enabling efficient transcription of multiple pages in a single forward pass.

📄 Abstract

Recently, end-to-end OCR models, exemplified by DeepSeek OCR, have once again thrust OCR into the spotlight. A widely held view is that employing a large language model (LLM) as the decoder allows the model to leverage the prior distribution of language, leading to improved OCR performance. However, the downside is equally evident: as the output sequence lengthens, the accumulated KV cache drives up memory consumption and progressively slows down generation. This stands in stark contrast to humans, who exhibit no such decline in efficiency during long-horizon copying tasks. In this technical report, we propose Unlimited OCR, a model designed to emulate human parsing working memory. Taking DeepSeek OCR as the baseline, we replace all attention layers in the decoder with our proposed Reference Sliding Window Attention (R-SWA), which reduces attention computation costs while maintaining a constant KV cache throughout the entire decoding process. By combining the high compression rate of DeepSeek OCR's encoder with our constant KV cache design, Unlimited OCR can transcribe dozens of pages of documents in a single forward pass under a standard maximum length of 32K. More importantly, R-SWA is a general-purpose parsing attention mechanism - beyond OCR, it is equally applicable to tasks such as ASR, translation, etc. Codes and model weights are publicly available at http://github.com/baidu/Unlimited-OCR.

📄 Abstract

AI systems can now operate Blender, follow instructions in complex video games, and manipulate objects with robot arms. What range of physical goals can today's AI systems achieve autonomously? Answering this question requires testing systems on tasks that demand different combinations of perception, memory, planning, and action. We introduce WorldLeague, a benchmark that compares AI systems on a common set of 60 simulated robot tasks by measuring progress toward each task's physical goal from the robot's trajectory. Robots explore scenes to gather evidence in WorldSeek, rearrange objects in WorldShape, and adapt to changes in WorldShift. Even when VLMs answer every final question correctly, they receive an Interactive Score of zero in 71.8\% of those episodes. WorldLeague gives Embodied AI, Physical AI, and Agentic AI a shared basis for comparing what AI systems achieve autonomously, from partial progress toward a physical goal to full goal completion. 📄 Abstract

Spatial intelligence, the ability to reason about 3D space from visual observations, becomes essential as multimodal large language models (MLLMs) move into the physical world. Beyond perceiving each view, it rests on two cornerstones of human cognition: fusing multiple views into one coherent 3D scene and mentally transforming that scene to new viewpoints as reasoning unfolds. Yet our spatial probes reveal that MLLMs largely lack both capabilities, even after spatial fine-tuning. We therefore present S-Mind, which internalizes multi-view geometry within the MLLM through a compact set of spatial tokens. It supervises these tokens to hold the fused 3D scene and re-emits them to re-express this scene from every viewpoint the reasoning adopts. Across four spatial benchmarks, S-Mind improves its Qwen3.5 backbones by up to 22.26 points and surpasses frontier general models such as GPT-6-Astra (+0.45), Kimi-K3.1-Pro (+1.97), and GLM-5.3-Flash (+7.80) in average accuracy. Moreover, the same probes confirm that it acquires both capabilities, reaching fusion and transformation accuracies of 65.11% and 82.17%, against at most 16.02% and 37.28% for the baselines. At inference, S-Mind also runs about 100x faster than a tool-based agent on the same backbone. Instilled through these spatial tokens during training, spatial capability becomes intrinsic to the backbone and persists even when the tokens are withheld, offering a promising path toward spatial intelligence in MLLMs.

🤖 AI Summary: Constraining residual stream mixing within the Birkhoff polytope via geometric contraction improves stability and scalability of hyper-connections in large-scale dense and mixture-of-experts model pretraining.

📄 Abstract

Manifold-constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) extends residual connections to multiple streams and stabilizes residual propagation by constraining stream-mixing residual matrices to the Birkhoff polytope, the set of doubly stochastic matrices. In this work, we investigate how to effectively mix residual streams within the Birkhoff polytope. We reveal a mixing trade-off: when under-mixed residual matrices stay close to the vertices of the Birkhoff polytope, hyper-connections will degenerate into a set of nearly independent streams; when over-mixed residual matrices are concentrated around the uniform barycenter of the polytope, streams will become less distinguishable from each other. We theoretically validate our findings by bounding the cumulative residual mixing, stream difference and gradient difference of mixing coefficients across layers. Motivated by these findings, we propose Contraction for Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (cmHC), an exact doubly stochastic method which geometrically contracts the Birkhoff polytope toward its interior. This contraction mitigates under-mixing by forcing the residual matrices to explore deeper into the polytope, with a provable stream mixing bound and no new parameterization. Moreover, we apply a moderate contraction at the shallow layers to prevent over-mixing, enabling stable gradient propagation on extremely large-scale model pretraining. Extensive experiments across dense and mixture-of-experts models at various parameter scales demonstrate the effectiveness and scalability of cmHC. We also develop fused operators for cmHC on Ascend NPUs, reducing hyper-connection execution latency by up to 44.8%.

🤖 AI Summary: Edge0 enables efficient mixture-of-experts inference on consumer hardware by predicting expert routing ahead of time and using recovery adapters to maintain quality with minimal active memory.

📄 Abstract

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) inference on consumer hardware is bounded by weight memory: a 35B-class model is 19.5GB at 4-bit, and sparsity shrinks the compute per token, not the bytes that must be held. Naive offloading to SSD does not help on its own, because layer N+1's experts must be chosen before layer N's output exists, so the reads cannot start early enough to hide behind compute. We present Edge0, a streaming MoE inference engine that closes the gap with a prerouter: a per-layer head predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead, and the prediction is consumed as the routing itself, so the staged expert set equals the routed set and nothing is dropped. An unmerged recovery LoRA, trained on the student path, pays back the quality lost to int4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s inside 3GiB of peak active memory, within a few points of its fp16 teacher on average across five public benchmarks.
