🤖 AI Summary: PDRVLA improves robot action generation by integrating a lightweight physical dynamics module that predicts future visual features with world-model supervision, avoiding online video generation latency.

📄 Abstract

Vision--Language--Action (VLA) models generate robot actions from visual observations and language instructions, but their understanding of physical dynamics remains limited. Video world models can capture scene dynamics, yet generating future videos introduces high latency into the control loop. We introduce PDRVLA, whose lightweight Physical Dynamics Module extracts physical tokens from vision--language features to condition action generation without online video generation. DAILYWEEKLY
📄 PDF 🐙 GitHub
🔥 100
⭐ 13

🤖 AI Summary: LOWN adaptively skips image processing during diffusion vision-language model decoding when predictions agree, reducing computation while preserving accuracy.

📄 Abstract

Diffusion vision-language models (DVLMs) process their entire input at every denoising step. Since visual tokens make up 89-97% of this input on the benchmarks we study, most of the decoding computation is spent repeatedly processing the same image. Visual token pruning methods partially relieve this burden by removing part of the visual tokens, but they lose the removed information and still process the remaining image at every step. To measure how often the image is actually needed, we compare LLaDA-V's predictions with and without the image at every decoding step and find that removing the image changes a token being unmasked at only 22.8% of steps on average and at as few as 7.9%. We therefore propose LOWN, a decoding method that decides at every step whether to process the image and how many tokens to unmask. LOWN predicts the masked tokens without the image and processes the image only when this prediction disagrees with the last image-conditioned prediction. Agreement certifies the earlier prediction for reuse, and the number of certified tokens sets how many are unmasked in that step. We further observe that the need for the image differs across benchmarks, so LOWN's adaptive decision is needed rather than a fixed schedule. Without training or a tuned threshold, LOWN retains 99.6% of LLaDA-V's score on ten benchmarks at 35.3% of the decoding FLOPs on average. LOWN also generalizes across LLaDA-based DVLMs, composes orthogonally with visual token pruning, and runs under key-value caching for further efficiency gains. Code and project page are available at https://hyeon9698.github.io/LOWN/

🤖 AI Summary: A-RSI improves recursive self-improvement by refreshing training tasks based on declining learning signals and using independently verifiable program-generated answers, raising benchmark performance without external labels.

📄 Abstract

Recursive self-improvement requires models not only to acquire useful training experience, but also to decide when their current experience has ceased to be useful. Existing methods often focus on what experience to generate next, while paying less attention to when fresh experience should be introduced. Many also construct training targets through self-consistency or majority voting, making supervision depend on the model's own uncertain predictions. We address these two questions separately: training dynamics determine when to acquire new experience, while the answers to new tasks remain independently checkable. A-RSI monitors reward variation and accuracy in training logs to refresh a task pool when its learning signal declines, without consulting validation or test scores. On the primary eleven-benchmark suite, A-RSI raises the performance average from 57.77 to 61.05 with 800 fresh tasks.

🤖 AI Summary: Jeva is a decision-native architecture that jointly enforces null-awareness, permutation invariance, option scalability, and cross-request caching for structured decision-making.

📄 Abstract

System one models enable direct, low-latency decision-making. Yet recent practice of predicting an answer from serialized text does not, by itself, respect the structure of a decision problem. We argue that current designs underemphasize four important structural requirements. (i) null-aware: Models should distinguish answer absence from uncertainty among candidates; (ii) permutation-invariant: Reordering alternatives should not change the decision; (iii) option-scalable: Growing candidate sets should not require redefining the output space; (iv) cacheable: Request-independent computation over recurring alternatives should be reusable across requests. We introduce Jeva, a decision-native architecture that addresses these requirements jointly. A shared encoder independently represents the request and each option, while a compact decision stack refines only the request state against immutable option memory. Symmetric aggregation yields permutation-equivariant probabilities; shared scoring accommodates variable-size candidate sets. A separate coverage estimate distinguishes answer absence from candidate competition, enabling explicit null outcomes. Request-independent option representations and layer projections support reuse across newly assembled sets. These mechanisms unify Choice, Noul, and Score outputs. Across evaluations, Jeva combines strong typed-decision performance with near-perfect null detection and no decision flips under any tested permutations. It scales to thousands of candidates and substantially reduces latency through cross-request reuse without changing decisions.

🤖 AI Summary: Joint-embedding predictive architectures trained on aligned multi-modal observations converge to shared representations with far greater efficiency than next-token prediction, demonstrating that positive view design—not scale alone—drives cross-modal alignment.

📄 Abstract

Whether models trained on different modalities converge to the same representation of the world is central to AI. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) conjectures that they do as they scale, but without a proof it offers neither a guarantee nor a recipe. We turn the hypothesis into a theorem. First, we prove a Platonic Representation Theorem: Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) trained separately on any number of modalities learn the same representation up to one rotation whenever their positive pairs are two observations of one scene--good views--and their misalignment is bounded otherwise. Second, we show that next-token prediction is a JEPA with bad views, parts of a single document, which explains why language models align with vision only through scale. Third, we validate the theory from 145k captions to the texts of 940M web images, and uncover a scaling law of alignment that predicts unseen runs within seed noise. Good views need 42 times fewer training samples than next-token prediction on the same corpus to reach the same alignment, and a 109M-parameter text JEPA aligns with DINOv2 and CLIP's image encoders better than every language model we evaluated, up to 72B parameters, and than CLIP's text encoder: next-token prediction is a remarkably inefficient route to platonic representations. As a showcase, one rotation turns separately trained encoders into an image captioner (CIDEr .838 on COCO). Even that rotation needs no pair: on the latest pair-free benchmark, our text JEPA beats every zero-shot accuracy reported with language models up to 8B parameters, e.g., 64\% against at most 56\% top-5 on ImageNet-100. Positive views, and not scale alone, are thus the lever to build platonic representations.

🤖 AI Summary: Prospective learning via Self-Retrospection Distillation uses hindsight experience to train foresight predictions, improving reinforcement learning when outcome rewards are uniform.

📄 Abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) turns agent experience into learning signals primarily through scalar outcome rewards after interaction. For group-relative objectives, however, this signal vanishes when all rollouts receive the same reward, even though their trajectories may reveal useful information about what the task requires and how the agent fails. We ask a complementary question: can hindsight teach an agent what it could have anticipated before acting? We introduce prospective learning, which uses post-hoc experience to supervise foresight predictions from the pre-interaction view, and instantiate it with Self-Retrospection Distillation (SRD). Intuitively, a completed trajectory reveals knowledge that would have been useful and pitfalls that should be avoided; SRD distills this privileged hindsight into trajectory-blind foresight of the same policy. Foresight serves only as a training target and need not be explicitly generated at inference time. Across 10 tool-integrated reasoning and long-horizon agentic tasks, SRD complements RLVR and self-distillation baselines with gains of up to 24.2 pp. Its advantage is especially pronounced when reward contrast is scarce: when 37--98% of rollout groups are reward-uniform across model scales, yet SRD can still exploit learning signal from sampled trajectories. In the 2B setting, where 98% of groups are all-failure, the RLVR training ends up at 0.0% success, while adding SRD reaches 60.6% under the same rollout budget. Our results suggest that post-hoc agent experience is useful not only for evaluating or improving behavior, but also for shaping predictive representations before available interaction.
