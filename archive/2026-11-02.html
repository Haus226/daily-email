FrameForge: Harnessing Priors of Video Foundation Models for Unified Image Generation

Published: 2026-10-27
DAILYMONTHLYWEEKLY
📄 PDF 🐙 GitHub
🔥 111
⭐ 3

🤖 AI Summary: FrameForge enables adaptive conditional image generation by modeling variable condition-target relationships through dynamic temporal experts and intermediate reasoning pathways in pretrained video models.

📄 Abstract

Unified conditional image generation requires a single model to preserve different aspects of a visual input while producing a target image that follows textual guidance. Pretrained video models provide a promising foundation by encoding visual transformations across time, yet existing approaches typically assign the condition and target to fixed temporal positions. We observe that condition-target relationships vary substantially across generation tasks, while video models naturally encode visual transformations at different temporal scales. This motivates us to model conditional generation through input-adaptive virtual temporal relations. We propose FrameForge, which captures two complementary aspects of such relations. Mixture of Temporal Experts (MoTE) models how far the target lies from the condition by dynamically selecting temporal RoPE experts with different virtual intervals. Chain of Temporal Reasoning (CoTR) models how the condition is transformed into the target through a latent intermediate state, with a reasoning-bottleneck attention mask encouraging information propagation through this pathway. Experiments on DreamBench, COCO2017, and GEdit-Bench show that FrameForge achieves superior performance in subject-driven generation, canny-to-image generation, and image editing among the compared methods.

🤖 AI Summary: R²Orch uses recursive self-play with learnable task selection and skill withdrawal to train orchestrators without hand-designed curricula, improving performance across reasoning and interactive tasks.

📄 Abstract

In recent years, agentic orchestration has been widely applied to solve complex human tasks. However, existing orchestration learning still faces key challenges, including hand-designed curricula, advantage collapse when a task's rollouts all reach the same outcome, and unreliable evaluation signals that judge each skill by one noisy score. To address these challenges, we propose R^2Orch, a new paradigm of Recursive Self-Play Orchestration: an inner loop of self-play that improves the orchestrator on self-chosen tasks, and an outer loop that improves how the inner loop improves. To support this paradigm, we introduce Recursive Learnability, a training objective that rewards a Proposer for choosing tasks whose rollouts keep reaching different outcomes as skills are withdrawn. Built on this objective, we further propose Skill Withdrawal, in which the outer loop withdraws each skill once a controlled evaluation within self-play shows that the orchestrator performs as well without it. Moreover, the two loops form one cycle: failures are distilled into skills, skills into parameters, and withdrawals lead the Proposer to new failures, so the orchestrator moves toward zero-skill deployment. Experimental results on twelve datasets show that R^2Orch consistently outperforms baselines across question answering, math, code, and interactive tasks. Our code is available at https://github.com/beita6969/R2Orch.

🤖 AI Summary: Co-designing a resource-heavy reasoning system with a lightweight predictive evaluator can reduce execution costs by filtering interactions, with efficiency governed by the density of useful interactions.

📄 Abstract

Progress toward artificial general intelligence (AGI) has largely relied on scaling a resource-intensive executive, System-2, through larger models, more data and compute, longer reasoning, and more agents. We ask whether the next stage may come instead from co-designing it with System-1, a lightweight, semantically aware predictor that evaluates candidate interactions before execution, so that System-2 acts only on those worth their cost. Our answer is a qualified yes. We study conditional interaction, a predict--gate--execute--update loop: System-1 estimates each candidate's utility, System-2 executes those admitted by an adaptive threshold, and the outcomes update System-1. The loop spans training, test-time reasoning, adaptive decision-making, and agentic tool use, with utility and cost defined by the application. Under ideal screening and equal execution costs, execution compute falls by a factor approaching 1/ρ_u, where ρ_u is the density of utility-relevant interactions (cost-weighted when costs differ); prediction errors, exploration, and distribution shift reduce the saving. We bound selection regret by predictor error and the utility margin. These results motivate our central hypothesis: ρ_u is a measurable coordinate of scaling efficiency and makes informative compute a better description of scaling than total compute.

🤖 AI Summary: REA unifies deliberative and reactive policies in a single runtime with asynchronous coordination, achieving high success rates with reduced control overhead across simulated and real robot tasks.

📄 Abstract

Foundation models are becoming remarkably reliable at high-level reasoning, yet they remain too slow and costly to serve as fine-grained controllers. Conversely, fast policies like VLAs and whole-body controllers offer more efficient reflexes, but struggle to generalize. While pairing a deliberative brain (foundation models) with a reactive cerebellum (fast policies) seems natural for embodied systems, two obstacles remain: methods are built and scored on incompatible stacks, and their asynchronous coordination is difficult. For example, an agent that calls the VLA in the agentic loop can stall the fast execution, while a VLA that calls the agent on errors tends to re-trigger fallbacks repeatedly or completely miss silent failures. To address this, we propose REA (Routed Embodied Agent), a unified runtime where a deliberative System 2, an embodied router System 1 and reactive System 0 policies cooperate under a single golden rule - no running policy waits on the brain. Instead of querying the brain at each failure, REA employs a route memory that stores each policy's measured success ahead of time, a boundary-triggered judge gate that consults the brain on policy execution, and a shared-budget escalation ladder for bounded recovery. Evaluated on 3,000 test episodes across 60 LIBERO-Pro tasks, REA reaches 95.9% success and beats the best single-agent baseline in its own policy pool (94.7%) while reducing control steps by 25% and control time by 17%. In a separate 100-episode RoboTwin 2.0 study, REA achieves 84% success with VLA policies and 86% with VLA+code, compared with 80% for the strongest single VLA baseline MEDex. These results demonstrate learned and programmatic policies operating within the same runtime. REA also transfers to BEHAVIOR-1K and a real Unitree G1 humanoid, achieving the highest observed success among the compared methods.

🤖 AI Summary: WeFair improves personalized query rewriting via fidelity-aware human feedback stages—demonstration, preference, and verification—to reduce intent drift and boost search effectiveness in short-video platforms.

📄 Abstract

Short-video search needs compact query rewriters that recover users' personalized intent from ambiguous queries under strict latency constraints. Existing LLM-based methods have improved rewriting by using synthetic reasoning and subsequent consumption-behavioral feedback. However, overemphasizing subsequent consumption can favor engaging content over the user's immediate need, causing intent drift even when rewritten queries are grounded in behavioral history. We propose WeFair, Fidelity-Aware Alignment from Human Feedback for Personalized Query Rewriting in WeChat short-video search. WeFair uses human feedback according to how directly it reflects personalized intent, assigning feedback fidelity to a progression from demonstration to preference to verification. We first mine actual queries and user reformulations from search sessions, and then screen them for contextual support. In the demonstration stage, a supervised fine-tuning curriculum progresses from large-scale context-to-query learning to reasoning and direct prediction on human reformulation and preservation. In the preference stage, direct preference optimization learns relative preferences from subsequent consumption feedback. In the verification stage, group relative policy optimization rewards exact agreement with reformulation or preservation targets to constrain intent drift. Further analysis shows that broad supervision benefits the full curriculum supervised training, while strict verification achieves better performance than auxiliary rewards. In offline experiments, WeFair-2B outperforms strong rewriting baselines in overall accuracy and intent preservation. An online A/B test shows relative increases of 2.593% in effective video-view rate and a 2.350% relative reduction in query reformulation rate. WeFair-2B has been deployed in the WeChat short-video search system, which serves over 100 million queries per day.

🤖 AI Summary: LARC enables continuous latent reasoning for unified multimodal models, improving image generation quality and enabling test-time scaling without discrete token bottlenecks.

📄 Abstract

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) achieve remarkable success on diverse multimodal understanding and generation tasks, yet they still struggle to effectively leverage language-based chain-of-thought reasoning for image generation. As a result, existing reasoning-augmented UMMs often fail to inherit the test-time scaling and fine-grained controllability observed in large language models. We attribute this failure to a fundamental modality mismatch: reasoning is performed in a discrete token space, whereas image generation operates in a continuous space. 📄 Abstract

Dense 3D tracking from monocular video requires establishing point correspondences over time, estimating their depth, and inferring their trajectories during occlusion. We show that strong pretrained 2D trackers and 3D geometry models provide an effective foundation: lifted trajectories, with occluded segments simply interpolated, already rival dedicated 3D trackers using the same geometry. 🤖 AI Summary: SimpleLoRIF uses gradient alignment to filter instruction data that silently degrades LLM safety, yielding a safer dataset that reduces adversarial success while preserving capabilities.

📄 Abstract

Fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) on instruction data can quietly erode their safety, even when no training example looks harmful. Because this safety-degrading data looks benign, standard content filters struggle to catch it. We tackle this problem by developing SimpleLoRIF, a scalable gradient embedding method that identifies fine-tuning examples whose gradients align with unsafe behavior. By simplifying a low-rank attribution method and restricting backpropagation to the final four transformer blocks, SimpleLoRIF can embed datasets on the order of a million examples, boosting gradient attribution from a small-scale analysis tool into a practical filter for full post-training datasets. We first validate it on emergent misalignment, where we find that gradients can connect insecure-code training data to the broadly harmful behaviors that appear after fine-tuning, with AUC as high as 0.865 in the gradient space of misaligned models. We then use SimpleLoRIF to build SafeTulu, a version of Tulu 3 with the 20% of examples most gradient-aligned with unsafe responses removed. Training on SafeTulu reduces GCG attack success rate by up to 22% across Qwen, Llama, and Olmo, outperforming both LLM-as-a-judge and prior gradient-based filtering, while largely preserving or even improving capability. Finally, we analyzed the removed data and identify possibly under-studied sources of safety degradation, such as tool-execution failures and narrative arithmetic. We release SafeTulu along with a Hugging Face Space that lets researchers build their own customized versions of SafeTulu to fine-tune on, such as by changing the removal fraction or filtering only certain subsets.

🤖 AI Summary: FreeToken is an edge-native Mixture-of-Experts serving system that dynamically maps computation and model state onto heterogeneous local hardware to run large open-weight models on personal machines.

📄 Abstract

Frontier open-weight models are increasingly available, but serving them still largely assumes datacenter infrastructure. We present FreeToken, an edge-native MoE serving system that treats a personal machine not as a small GPU, but as a unified, elastic inference platform. FreeToken co-designs the full serving stack, including model layout and loading, expert residency, CPU--GPU execution, agentic state reuse, and runtime memory management, around two realities of local AI: agent workloads continuously change their execution pattern, and edge hardware exposes heterogeneous resources whose balance differs from machine to machine. Rather than committing to a fixed offloading strategy, FreeToken continuously maps computation and model state onto the resources actually available. FreeToken supports more than 20 MoE models and real coding and tool-using agents across hardware ranging from an 8GB laptop GPU to a single workstation GPU. More importantly, it changes what these machines can practically serve, from a 35B model on a laptop to a 284B model on a gaming desktop and the 753B GLM-5.2 on a single workstation GPU. FreeToken turns open weights into deployable local software, making the machines users already own a practical platform for frontier-scale intelligence. We release the system at flashml.ai.

🤖 AI Summary: Unlimited OCR introduces Reference Sliding Window Attention to eliminate growing memory consumption during long-sequence OCR tasks, enabling efficient transcription of multiple pages in a single forward pass.

📄 Abstract

Recently, end-to-end OCR models, exemplified by DeepSeek OCR, have once again thrust OCR into the spotlight. A widely held view is that employing a large language model (LLM) as the decoder allows the model to leverage the prior distribution of language, leading to improved OCR performance. However, the downside is equally evident: as the output sequence lengthens, the accumulated KV cache drives up memory consumption and progressively slows down generation. This stands in stark contrast to humans, who exhibit no such decline in efficiency during long-horizon copying tasks. In this technical report, we propose Unlimited OCR, a model designed to emulate human parsing working memory. Taking DeepSeek OCR as the baseline, we replace all attention layers in the decoder with our proposed Reference Sliding Window Attention (R-SWA), which reduces attention computation costs while maintaining a constant KV cache throughout the entire decoding process. By combining the high compression rate of DeepSeek OCR's encoder with our constant KV cache design, Unlimited OCR can transcribe dozens of pages of documents in a single forward pass under a standard maximum length of 32K. More importantly, R-SWA is a general-purpose parsing attention mechanism - beyond OCR, it is equally applicable to tasks such as ASR, translation, etc. Codes and model weights are publicly available at http://github.com/baidu/Unlimited-OCR.

🤖 AI Summary: Edge0 enables efficient mixture-of-experts inference on consumer hardware by predicting expert routing ahead of time and using recovery adapters to maintain quality with minimal active memory.

📄 Abstract

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) inference on consumer hardware is bounded by weight memory: a 35B-class model is 19.5GB at 4-bit, and sparsity shrinks the compute per token, not the bytes that must be held. Naive offloading to SSD does not help on its own, because layer N+1's experts must be chosen before layer N's output exists, so the reads cannot start early enough to hide behind compute. We present Edge0, a streaming MoE inference engine that closes the gap with a prerouter: a per-layer head predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead, and the prediction is consumed as the routing itself, so the staged expert set equals the routed set and nothing is dropped. An unmerged recovery LoRA, trained on the student path, pays back the quality lost to int4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s inside 3GiB of peak active memory, within a few points of its fp16 teacher on average across five public benchmarks. An 8B tier runs on the same framework, and the framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open source.

🤖 AI Summary: HairDeck generates stylized hair cards and spring-bone animations as unordered token sets using a diffusion transformer, improving accuracy and speed over prior methods.

📄 Abstract

The hair of stylized characters in games and avatars is built from hair cards: artist-placed ribbons whose geometry is the visible hair, animated by spring bones. Existing image-based methods produce realistic hair or a single fused mesh, while CHARM, the only generator of stylized hair cards, decodes a hairstyle autoregressively as a long sequence of control points. Observing that a card hairstyle is an unordered set of artist-defined primitives, we propose HairDeck, which generates it as one: each card is a single token, and a set diffusion transformer denoises all cards together. From a single image, HairDeck generates the cards and the spring-bone chains that drive them, yielding an animatable asset that exports to ready-to-use downstream formats. On CHARM's own test set, it lowers Chamfer distance by 44% and card-orientation error by more than half while running 33 times faster, and from single images it outperforms CHARM, image-to-3D pipelines and strand-based methods. 📄 Abstract

Compact OCR-specific vision-language models achieve strong document parsing performance, but often rely on costly supervision and focus primarily on visual-text reconstruction. We introduce Xiaomi-OCR-0, a unified 0.8B model for document parsing and OCR-centric understanding. We build an approximately 170M-sample OCR-centric corpus using an automated data engine that combines expert consensus, render-based verification, and targeted synthesis. Starting from Qwen3.5-0.8B, our progressive training recipe combines Q-Mask-based text anchoring, continued pretraining, and mixed-task reinforcement learning (Mix-RL). Ablations further show that, with sufficient parsing training, OCR-centric understanding supervision provides additional gains for document parsing. 🤖 AI Summary: PagedAttention algorithm and vLLM system enhance the throughput of large language models by efficiently managing memory and reducing waste in the key-value cache.

📄 Abstract

High throughput serving of large language models (LLMs) requires batching sufficiently many requests at a time. However, existing systems struggle because the key-value cache (KV cache) memory for each request is huge and grows and shrinks dynamically. When managed inefficiently, this memory can be significantly wasted by fragmentation and redundant duplication, limiting the batch size. To address this problem, we propose PagedAttention, an attention algorithm inspired by the classical virtual memory and paging techniques in operating systems. On top of it, we build vLLM, an LLM serving system that achieves (1) near-zero waste in KV cache memory and (2) flexible sharing of KV cache within and across requests to further reduce memory usage. Our evaluations show that vLLM improves the throughput of popular LLMs by 2-4times with the same level of latency compared to the state-of-the-art systems, such as FasterTransformer and Orca. The improvement is more pronounced with longer sequences, larger models, and more complex decoding algorithms. vLLM's source code is publicly available at https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm

🤖 AI Summary: MOSS-Image is an open diffusion transformer that combines high-fidelity synthesis with intent-aware prompt enhancement to generate text-rich, layout-coherent images, achieving state-of-the-art open-weight results on long-form text and layout benchmarks.

📄 Abstract

Recent closed-source image generation systems can produce diverse visual content from simple compositions to information-dense graphics, yet open-weight text-to-image models still struggle with dense text and intricate layouts. To narrow this gap, we introduce MOSS-Image, an open generation system that combines high-fidelity visual synthesis with intent-aware composition. First, we pretrain a 5B single-stream diffusion transformer (DiT) from scratch with a carefully designed recipe, enabling bilingual long-form text rendering and precise layout control. Second, we develop an intention-aware agentic prompt enhancement (PE) module that translates diverse user requests into detailed content and layout specifications. Together, they enable MOSS-Image to generate text-rich and layout-coherent images. We further introduce LongLongTextBench (LLTB) for evaluating long-form English and Chinese text rendering, and RealLayoutArena (RLA) for end-to-end layout-rich generation. MOSS-Image achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-weight models on LLTB, with an average text coverage of 96.3%. On RLA, MOSS-Image ranks second in the end-to-end system comparison, while its backbone ranks first among generators evaluated with shared enhanced prompts. We will release the model, training recipe, and evaluation framework to support further research and promote the text-to-image community.
