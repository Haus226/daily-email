Incentivizing Self-Learning in Large Language Models via Reinforcement Learning

Published: 2026-10-29
DAILY

No GitHub

⭐ 0

🤖 AI Summary: (No summary available)

📄 Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) draw on parametric knowledge and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to solve knowledge-intensive tasks. Yet problem solving in unfamiliar domains calls for tightly coupled knowledge acquisition and reasoning, beyond factual retrieval and aggregation or learning solely from a preassembled context. We study self-learning -- autonomously retrieving, studying, and applying knowledge from long-form reference materials on the fly. To investigate this capability, we formulate a textbook-based mathematical problem-solving task. LLMs must navigate textbook exposition, learn and apply relevant principles, and construct proofs for exercises, with reference solutions withheld. We introduce a BookNavigator interface to support this human-inspired study process and incentivize self-learning through reinforcement learning (RL) with fine-grained rewards. Experiments show that our method improves performance across in-domain and out-of-domain textbooks and subjects, raising Intern-S2-Preview-35B's overall proof accuracy from 51.5% to 79.5% and outperforming RL-trained textbook-free and fixed-retrieval baselines. These gains extend to complex long-context benchmarks involving different task types, with improvements over the base model of 1.7 and 2.3 percentage points on BrowseComp-Plus and CL-bench, respectively. Together, these findings suggest that RL can foster a domain-agnostic self-learning strategy, highlighting the potential of self-learning from long-form corpora to strengthen broader problem-solving capabilities on complex, long-context tasks.

🤖 AI Summary: A large-scale corpus of structured proof-supervision records derived from university mathematics textbooks improves language model mathematical reasoning by 16.7 percentage points over a base model when used for fine-tuning.

📄 Abstract

Mathematical supervision for language models is typically flattened into isolated problem-solution pairs, discarding the curricular organization and evidential structure of the original sources. We introduce TOME-Math (Textbook-Oriented Mathematical Evidence), a large-scale corpus that converts 2,118 textbooks spanning the core areas of university-level mathematics subjects into approximately 380K structured proof-supervision records while preserving chapter order, proposition-proof links, dependency references, and source provenance. Leveraging these structural signals, we explore curriculum learning across multiple mathematical subjects and characterize scaling patterns with supervision volume and source breadth. At full scale, fine-tuning on TOME-Math raises the unweighted mean score across our held-out evaluations and established public benchmarks by 16.7 percentage points over the base model, while exceeding all three public instruction-data baselines evaluated in aggregate. Our findings highlight structured textbooks as both a scalable source of mathematical supervision and an empirical framework for studying the impact of data organization on mathematical reasoning. We will release the corpus to facilitate further research on structured mathematical supervision.

🤖 AI Summary: CogBattery compares open-weight language models to humans across cognitive tasks, revealing that matching effect directions does not imply human-like error patterns or confidence calibration.

📄 Abstract

Human cognitive tasks let us compare language models with humans under controlled conditions. Yet a model can reproduce the direction of a human effect while making different errors. CogBattery evaluates 54 open-weight language models on text adaptations of 13 cognitive paradigms, comparing each with human data from its source study. Like humans, models respond more to n-back lures than to controls, yet they make far more false alarms on ordinary non-targets. Most models' reported confidence separates correct from incorrect answers less well than humans' does, and most always choose the same wager, including models that classify accurately. These patterns span many model families. Across tasks, model scores share two dimensions. The first is closely tied to scale, yet larger models are not consistently closer to humans. Predefined cognitive groupings show no reliable predictive advantage over random groupings of the same size. These results show that matching human effect directions does not establish human-like behavior. CogBattery provides condition-level human references for testing errors and confidence reports in new models.

🔥 128

🤖 AI Summary: Wave Forcing enables efficient continuous long-video generation with consistent appearance and natural motion through co-designed generation, training, and execution, achieving high throughput and quality.

📄 Abstract

Real-time long-video generation requires continuous output with consistent appearance and natural motion. Existing chunk-wise autoregressive methods face challenges in both generation efficiency and long-video quality: serialized denoising and history-cache refresh require two forward passes even with one-step denoising, and longer rollouts can degrade quality and cause camera drift. We propose Wave Forcing (WF), which co-designs generation, training, and execution for continuous video output with multi-step denoising. WF uses chunk-wise causal attention to access earlier, cleaner chunks before their denoising is complete, and distributes staggered denoising and cache-refresh stages across full-model replicas to achieve 1F1C: one output chunk per concurrent forward round after pipeline fill. To adapt the model to this continuous generation process, we develop a three-stage training procedure that successively introduces mixed-noise denoising, motion supervision, and causal adaptation using self-generated history. To execute this process efficiently, Wave Runtime (WaveRT) uses Wave Parallelism (WP) for early K/V delivery and communication-computation overlap, together with optimized kernels and pipelined VAE decoding. Experiments on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B show that 5-step WF maintains overall quality comparable to Rolling Forcing while achieving higher Dynamic Degree on both short and long videos. Our system efficiency experiments show that 4-step WF achieves 147.6 DiT FPS and 134.8 end-to-end FPS on eight H200 GPUs, yielding 7.07x the single-GPU end-to-end throughput. In DiT-only tests with matched SageAttention+W8A8 backends, denoising steps, and GPU counts, WP delivers up to 2.53x the throughput of the faster of the Ulysses and Ring context-parallel baselines across six model scales (1.3B-14B).

🤖 AI Summary: Edge0 enables efficient mixture-of-experts inference on consumer hardware by predicting expert routing ahead of time and using recovery adapters to maintain quality with minimal active memory.

📄 Abstract

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) inference on consumer hardware is bounded by weight memory: a 35B-class model is 19.5GB at 4-bit, and sparsity shrinks the compute per token, not the bytes that must be held. Naive offloading to SSD does not help on its own, because layer N+1's experts must be chosen before layer N's output exists, so the reads cannot start early enough to hide behind compute. We present Edge0, a streaming MoE inference engine that closes the gap with a prerouter: a per-layer head predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead, and the prediction is consumed as the routing itself, so the staged expert set equals the routed set and nothing is dropped. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s inside 3GiB of peak active memory, within a few points of its fp16 teacher on average across five public benchmarks. An 8B tier runs on the same framework, and the framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open source.

🤖 AI Summary: MACaQ is an MXFP4 W4A4 post-training quantization method that quantizes each linear layer independently using online activation transforms and Hessian-aware refinement, reducing cost while preserving accuracy across language and diffusion models.

📄 Abstract

Weight-activation quantization can reduce the memory and compute cost of neural-network inference, but recent methods often require expensive optimization and architecture-specific propagation of basis transforms across layers. We introduce MACaQ, an MXFP4 W4A4 post-training quantization method that treats every linear layer independently. MACaQ applies a per-channel scale and a block-Hadamard transform online to each layer's activations, while absorbing the corresponding inverse transforms into its weights. Retaining these transforms online avoids cross-layer dependencies and enables a simple streaming implementation of the quantization process, in which only one layer must reside in memory at a time. To improve over round-to-nearest quantization, we introduce Flip-Refinement, a Hessian-aware coordinate-descent procedure that greedily moves individual FP4 codes to adjacent representable values. Despite executing the activation transforms at inference time, MACaQ adds only 2.3% end-to-end overhead over pure MXFP4 on Qwen3-32B in vLLM. MACaQ reaches lower perplexity than DuQuant++ and MR-GPTQ, and already matches their best perplexity on Llama-3-8B in 11 times less quantization time. We further apply MACaQ without architecture-specific modifications to an image diffusion model, illustrating the generality of its layer-local design and show quantization of a 122B parameter model in 64 minutes, using only 32.5GB GPU RAM.

📄 Abstract

Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) offer complementary routes to post-training. For visual agents, however, tool use presents two related challenges. We introduce Reframe, a post-training framework that treats the observation state, rather than only the output distribution, as an object of alignment. First, Ground-Drop-GRPO (GD-GRPO) trains spatial and temporal specialists with evidence-aware rewards and stochastic removal of the original visual input, encouraging them to acquire grounded evidence and use the returned observations.
