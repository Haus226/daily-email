cmHC: Balancing Stream Mixing in Hyper-Connections via Geometric Contraction

Published: 2026-11-20
DAILYWEEKLY
📄 PDF 🐙 GitHub
🔥 49
⭐ 0

🤖 AI Summary: Constraining residual stream mixing within the Birkhoff polytope via geometric contraction improves stability and scalability of hyper-connections in large-scale dense and mixture-of-experts model pretraining.

📄 Abstract

Manifold-constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) extends residual connections to multiple streams and stabilizes residual propagation by constraining stream-mixing residual matrices to the Birkhoff polytope, the set of doubly stochastic matrices. In this work, we investigate how to effectively mix residual streams within the Birkhoff polytope. We reveal a mixing trade-off: when under-mixed residual matrices stay close to the vertices of the Birkhoff polytope, hyper-connections will degenerate into a set of nearly independent streams; when over-mixed residual matrices are concentrated around the uniform barycenter of the polytope, streams will become less distinguishable from each other. We theoretically validate our findings by bounding the cumulative residual mixing, stream difference and gradient difference of mixing coefficients across layers. Motivated by these findings, we propose Contraction for Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (cmHC), an exact doubly stochastic method which geometrically contracts the Birkhoff polytope toward its interior. This contraction mitigates under-mixing by forcing the residual matrices to explore deeper into the polytope, with a provable stream mixing bound and no new parameterization. Moreover, we apply a moderate contraction at the shallow layers to prevent over-mixing, enabling stable gradient propagation on extremely large-scale model pretraining. Extensive experiments across dense and mixture-of-experts models at various parameter scales demonstrate the effectiveness and scalability of cmHC. We also develop fused operators for cmHC on Ascend NPUs, reducing hyper-connection execution latency by up to 44.8%.

Non-penetrating World Models

Published: 2026-11-20
DAILYWEEKLY
📄 PDF 🐙 GitHub
🔥 38

🤖 AI Summary: A benchmark and lightweight framework leverage hidden states from frozen diffusion transformers to detect and prevent object penetration in interactive video world models, reducing physical violations and generalizing zero-shot.

📄 Abstract

We study a new problem of preventing object penetration in interactive video world models. To understand how existing models handle this, we introduce NoPeBench, a benchmark that evaluates how world models respond to physical obstacles. Our evaluation reveals that none of the open-source models can reliably prevent such failures. Surprisingly, however, these models already encode the information needed to predict such violations. We find that the hidden states of diffusion transformers (DiTs) can predict whether a candidate action will penetrate an obstacle. Building on this, we introduce NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that detects and prevents penetration. Specifically, we train a small prediction head on top of a frozen DiT's hidden states to read out if a candidate action is safe or not. Unsafe actions are then replaced with safe ones that best preserve the original intent. Despite its simplicity, NoPeWorld substantially reduces the physical violation rate, averaged over six video world models, from 74% to 16%. Remarkably, our method shows zero-shot generalization to open-domain imagery, third-person viewpoints, and moving obstacles. We publicly release our code, the NoPeBench benchmark, and model weights. Project Page: https://nopeworld.github.io .

We introduce Clover-35B-A3B-Preview (Clover), a mixture-of-experts diffusion language model with 35 billion total parameters and 3 billion activated parameters, supporting thinking-mode generation. Reading the Unreadable: Text-Aware Image Super-Resolution Needs Reasoning

Published: 2026-11-11
DAILYWEEKLY
📄 PDF 🐙 GitHub
🔥 15
⭐ 0

🤖 AI Summary: (No summary available)

📄 Abstract

Text-aware image super-resolution (TAISR) aims to recover high-resolution images from low-resolution inputs while preserving textual content. Existing methods treat this as a visual restoration problem, yet severely degraded text often cannot be read from local visual evidence alone. Humans instead read it by reasoning over image context, logical patterns, and prior knowledge. This raises a fundamental question: Does TAISR need reasoning? To answer this, we introduce two diagnostic tools. The first, ReasonText, is a human-curated benchmark that separates reasoning-required text from locally recoverable text. The second, Ground-Truth Text-Crop Accuracy (GTTCA), is a recognition-based metric that measures text restoration quality without text detection errors. Evaluating twelve recent super-resolution (SR) models, we find a consistent and substantial performance drop on reasoning-required text, revealing a systematic blind spot. Two observations point to a remedy. One is that text-conditioned SR models restore text substantially better when given ground-truth text. The other is that multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can read degraded text in low-resolution images through reasoning. We therefore propose Reasoning Transfer via Captioning (RTC), a training-free plug-in that uses an external MLLM to infer the degraded text and feeds the resulting caption to a text-conditioned SR model. RTC consistently improves all six text-conditioned SR models we evaluate, especially on reasoning-required text. Surprisingly, a real-world SR model with RTC can even outperform the TAISR model with RTC, suggesting that reasoning should be a core design axis for future TAISR.

MemOnce: A Scalable Memory Architecture for Large Language Models

Published: 2026-11-20
DAILYWEEKLY
📄 PDF 🐙 GitHub
🔥 9
⭐ 11

🤖 AI Summary: (No summary available)

📄 Abstract

Deploying large language models (LLMs) in long-horizon and continual interactions requires maintaining reusable memory states for future inference, posing challenges for existing architectures. Standard Transformers accumulate layer-wise, token-level KV caches, incurring linear GPU memory growth and quadratic computational complexity as the context expands. In contrast, linear-time architectures compress history into fixed-size states, but struggle to retain fine-grained details. We introduce MemOnce, a foundation architecture that learns scalable memory states for language models. MemOnce is based on two core designs: (a) Memory encoder-decoder: it caches only a small subset of final-layer hidden states as compact memory representations and dynamically decodes them back into KV caches for attention layers; (b) Memory sparse attention: for each continual input, MemOnce adaptively retrieves relevant memory representations, with the sparse indices computed only once across layers. Coupling these two designs enables efficient on-the-fly memory loading and KV cache decoding, drastically reducing the GPU memory footprint and achieving linear-time complexity. We conduct extensive experiments to show that MemOnce outperforms Transformers and other architectures across varying model sizes, context lengths, and training paradigms (pretraining and post-training adaptation). In particular, adapting Qwen3.5-4B with MemOnce reduces memory state cache by 1313times and prefill latency by 6.9times at 1M context, and improves throughput by 3.5times and overall memory performance by +5.77%. Finally, MemOnce generalizes zero-shot to downstream representation tasks, achieving competitive results against specialized models.

🤖 AI Summary: Unlimited OCR introduces Reference Sliding Window Attention to eliminate growing memory consumption during long-sequence OCR tasks, enabling efficient transcription of multiple pages in a single forward pass.

📄 Abstract

Recently, end-to-end OCR models, exemplified by DeepSeek OCR, have once again thrust OCR into the spotlight. A widely held view is that employing a large language model (LLM) as the decoder allows the model to leverage the prior distribution of language, leading to improved OCR performance. However, the downside is equally evident: as the output sequence lengthens, the accumulated KV cache drives up memory consumption and progressively slows down generation. This stands in stark contrast to humans, who exhibit no such decline in efficiency during long-horizon copying tasks. In this technical report, we propose Unlimited OCR, a model designed to emulate human parsing working memory. Taking DeepSeek OCR as the baseline, we replace all attention layers in the decoder with our proposed Reference Sliding Window Attention (R-SWA), which reduces attention computation costs while maintaining a constant KV cache throughout the entire decoding process. By combining the high compression rate of DeepSeek OCR's encoder with our constant KV cache design, Unlimited OCR can transcribe dozens of pages of documents in a single forward pass under a standard maximum length of 32K. More importantly, R-SWA is a general-purpose parsing attention mechanism - beyond OCR, it is equally applicable to tasks such as ASR, translation, etc. Codes and model weights are publicly available at http://github.com/baidu/Unlimited-OCR.

Persistent spatial understanding is essential for embodied agents to operate reliably in environments they repeatedly encounter. 📄 Abstract

Prompt enhancement (PE) refines concise user prompts into detailed descriptions to improve the compositional aesthetics of generated images while preserving user intent. A common practice constructs supervised fine-tuning (SFT) pairs by deriving short, user-like prompts from dense captions, implicitly assuming that captions that improve image generation also provide effective supervision. In this paper, however, we uncover a counterintuitive finding: PE models trained on caption pairs with substantial generation gains perform comparably to those trained on pairs with only marginal gains. 📄 Abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models enable unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to follow natural-language instructions, yet still fail frequently in closed-loop flight. Correcting these failures requires repairs, i.e., actions that avoid them at the states the policy itself reaches, which human pilots cannot label at scale and rule-based planners cannot supply reliably. In this paper, we present AeroRSI, a recursive self-improvement framework in which an aerial navigation policy iteratively learns from its own failures. Our key insight is that a failing policy should judge its own repairs by flying on from them. Judged in this way, its sampled actions and even rule-based proposals that hurt it when imitated directly become reliable supervision. Moreover, an episode terminates at its first failure, concealing subsequent failures until the earlier ones are repaired. AeroRSI thus renews its supervision in every round, anchoring the failures of the current policy, verifying repairs by its continuation, and distilling them while keeping its behavior elsewhere. On TravelUAV, three rounds raise the success rate of a supervised 2B-parameter policy from 46.4% to 67.5% and reduce collisions by half, surpassing the previous state of the art by 10.6 points from the same initialization without extra human labels or inference cost. 📄 Abstract

Controllable real-world video generation models can condition on a rendered graybox proxy video, which fixes layout, motion and camera. Coding agents can build such videos by reconstructing a 4D scene in Blender, yet no benchmark checks both these videos and the long, multi-shot scenes behind them. We introduce VibePrevisBench, a benchmark for 4D scene reconstruction: an agent turns a brief into an executable 4D scene and renders its proxy video. Its 242 tasks span four input kinds, including scripts, photos, continuations, and reference videos. Each delivery is scored along two evaluation dimensions. Rule-based Scene Check re-runs the code and compares the exported 4D scene with hidden ground truth facts in nine dimensions. VLM-based Screen Check asks a VLM judge whether the facts that pass can be recognized in the proxy video. We analyze the evaluation results and trajectories of 11 frontier coding agents and identify three key observations. First, multimodal understanding separates the agents, especially in understanding space and camera motion. Second, all agents seem to already master basic camera work and staging, and fail at challenging requests. Third, agents inspect their own renders but fail to correct their errors. Agents thus need better visual understanding of space and camera, explicit checks for challenging requests, and item-by-item verification of renders against the brief.

🤖 AI Summary: Kling's multimodal understanding stack links caption density, perceptual text encoding, reward modeling, and evaluation protocols to improve controllable video generation.

📄 Abstract

Video generation has progressed from isolated clips to coherent short-form audiovisual sequences, and the bottleneck is shifting from rendering to semantic control. This report summarizes the observations and insights on multimodal understanding gained from successive generations of Kling. We argue that generation quality is the projection of multimodal understanding through the entire data--conditioning--alignment--evaluation chain, and we examine this chain module by module. Kling-MLLM, a shared multimodal foundation model that unifies image, video, audio, synchronized audio--video, and interleaved inputs, is specialized for each downstream module. Because a DiT is trained on far fewer training samples than a language model, we treat captioning as a semantic contract that maximizes description density, and extend it to reference-grounded captioning that makes reference bindings explicit. Prompt enhancement translates user intent into this caption distribution: basic PE is derived from captions of compelling videos and refined with text-level and generator-in-the-loop reinforcement learning, while agentic PE refines the story and retrieves compelling examples. We show that a reliable text encoder comes from perceptual training rather than from stronger language ability, and that candidates can be screened from text alone before end-to-end training. Reward modeling pairs a general reward model for explainable, localized critiques with a decision reward model for fast multidimensional scoring, treating perceptual bandwidth as a first-class design parameter. Finally, we review task-specific automatic evaluation, focusing on protocols that expose scoring shortcuts and help trace failures to their upstream source. We hope these lessons offer practical guidance for building the understanding stack behind controllable video generation.
